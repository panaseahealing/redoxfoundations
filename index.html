Cellular Intelligence™

Your body already knows what to do.

Somewhere in our forties, many of us start to notice things. We don't bounce back the way we used to. Sleep changes. Our energy isn't what it was. And more often than not, we're told it's just our age.

I've heard that line so many times, in my own life and in my Bowen room. And I've come to believe there's more to the story.

Right now, as you read this, trillions of cells in your body are sensing, communicating and responding to what's happening around them. They aren't working alone. They're part of an extraordinary network, constantly passing messages back and forth so your body can keep its balance and adapt to whatever life brings.

I think of it as Cellular Intelligence.

Underneath so much of that conversation is something most of us were never taught about: redox signalling. It's one of the basic ways our cells talk to each other and respond to the world around them.

This isn't something we add to the body.
It's something your body already does, every second of every day.

The more I learnt, the more my thinking changed. For years, like so many of us, I'd asked, "What should I be taking?" Then I found myself asking a different question.

"How well are my cells talking to each other?"

That one question changed how I think about getting older. It isn't about fighting our age or chasing the next quick fix. It's about caring for the body we have, from the inside out, so we can keep doing the things we love, with the people we love, for as long as we can.

The basics, without the jargon

01

The messengers

Your cells naturally make tiny molecules as part of everyday life. Scientists once thought of them as waste. Now they understand that some of them act as messengers, helping cells know when to respond and adapt.

02

The balance

Healthy cells keep these messages in balance, a bit like a good conversation where everyone gets a turn. Researchers are actively studying how that balance changes as we get older.

03

The whole picture

How you sleep, move, eat, handle stress and connect with the people around you all shape the world your cells live in. There's no single answer, and anyone who tells you otherwise isn't being straight with you.

My story

It all started with Mum

When Redox came into our lives in 2018, it wasn't for me. It was for my mum. She was 81, I was turning 50, and my husband and I had been living with Mum and caring for her since 2011. She'd seen specialist after specialist, and we'd tried just about everything we could think of to help her.

I was working night shifts on the railway so I could take Mum to her appointments during the day. One night a colleague, an electrician, asked why I liked night shift. When I told him about Mum, he mentioned something his family had been using for his mother-in-law, who was around the same age. My first thought was, "Oh no, here we go again." But I was curious enough to listen, and his wife patiently answered all my questions about redox signalling.

What caught me was learning that our bodies make these signalling molecules naturally, and that they're part of how our cells talk to each other. After years of chasing one thing after another, the idea of supporting the body as a whole made real sense to me. Mum decided to give it a go, and I decided that if she was taking it, I wanted to try it for myself too.

Then I found Bowen

About a year later, three little things pointed me somewhere I never expected. Mum was having treatment at the Perth Bowen Clinic, and I picked up a booklet in the waiting room. On the way home Mum said, "You need to study that." I told her she was crazy. Anatomy was my worst subject at school!

Then I took our old Blue Heeler, Muppet, in for some Bowen for her hips. On our third visit I asked the trainer to tell me more. Most of it went straight over my head, until she said it works deep at the cellular level. By then I'd been learning about cellular health for a while, including training as a cellular health coach with Dr Gary Samuelson. Something clicked.

I'd never had a single Bowen session myself, but I enrolled anyway. When it came to naming my practice, I chose Panasea Healing, a little play on the word panacea with ASEA tucked inside, because it brings together the two things I love, Bowen Therapy and cellular health. Today I work with people, many of them women in midlife, who are often where I once was: tired, putting themselves last and accepting how they feel as just part of getting older.

Almost 58, and still curious

In June this year we said goodbye to my beautiful Mum, just three weeks short of her 89th birthday. She shared our home for many years, and our boys had the gift of really knowing their Oma. I miss her terribly, and so much of what I do today began with her.

I'm not saying I have a perfect body or never have a hard day. But I've learnt how much I'd been quietly putting up with, the way so many of us do while we raise families, work and care for everyone else. Redox has been part of my every day since 2018, alongside good food, water, movement and all the other things I try (not always perfectly!) to get right.

We spend so many years putting ourselves last. Then one day we realise it's our turn. I don't just want to get older. I want to age well and enjoy the life I have ahead of me. And if sharing my story helps someone else get curious about their own health, I'm happy to keep telling it.

I'm not a doctor and I won't pretend to be, and everyone's journey is different. What I can do is share what I've learned, point you to the research and be straight with you. If you'd like to chat, that conversation is yours to start, and I'll never push.

The honest questions

Things a reasonable, sceptical person would ask.

Is this just antioxidant marketing with a new name? +

It's an important and reasonable question. The difference between antioxidant supplements and redox signalling support is one researchers have specifically looked at, and it matters. Traditional antioxidant thinking tries to neutralise reactive oxygen species. Current redox biology takes a different view. The goal isn't to get rid of these molecules, but to support the body's ability to use them as signals, working with what's already there rather than simply adding more in. The peer-reviewed research linked on this page explores that difference in depth.

Did anyone actually discover this, or is it hype? +

Redox signalling was recognised at the highest levels of science decades before any product existed. It's been studied for roughly forty years, with tens of thousands of peer-reviewed papers behind it. What no one could do for a long time was hold these molecules stable outside the body, in a form the body recognises. Solving that took one company around sixteen years of research, and the first stable, drinkable form was released in 2009. The science is well established, and the PubMed search linked on this page shows just how much of it there is.

Why haven't I heard of this if the research is so established? +

Most cellular biology research lives in academic journals the public rarely sees. Redox signalling has been studied for decades in those settings, but because it doesn't map neatly onto one product, it hasn't had the mainstream attention that vitamins or omega-3s have. It isn't obscure within science. It just hasn't been explained well for everyday people yet.

I'm already healthy. Is this even relevant to me? +

The research on redox signalling isn't mainly about illness. It's about how cells communicate and keep their balance in all conditions, including healthy ones. So the question isn't so much "am I unwell?" as "how well is my body's communication network running?" That's worth wondering about wherever you are in your health.
